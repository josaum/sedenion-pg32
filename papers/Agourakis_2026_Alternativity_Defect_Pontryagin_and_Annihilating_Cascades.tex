\begin{abstract}
In every associative algebra---including the real field $\R$, the complex field $\C$, the quaternion division ring $\Ha$, and the matrix ring $M_n(\C)$---two linear filters $w_1, w_2$ that annihilate each other ($w_1 \cdot w_2 = 0$) produce identically zero output when cascaded on any input signal $x$, since $(x \cdot w_1)\cdot w_2 = x \cdot (w_1 \cdot w_2) = 0$. Similarly, in classical array processing, inverting or equalizing a 16-dimensional channel operator $L(u)$ requires $\mathcal{O}(16^3)$ Gaussian elimination or Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). In this paper, we show that crossing the Hurwitz and Moufang thresholds into the 16-dimensional Cayley--Dickson sedenion algebra $\Se = \Oc \oplus \Oc e_8$ overturns both paradigms through five exact algebraic laws, all machine-checked with \emph{zero axioms} in the Lean~4 theorem prover (\texttt{204/204} theorems): (i) \emph{The Symmetric Alternativity-Defect Calculus:} left and right alternativity fail by the exact same symmetric linear operator $N_u(y) \triangleq u(uy) - u^2 y = (yu)u - y u^2 = ([a,b,d]_{\Oc}, -[a,b,c]_{\Oc})$, which governs the normal operator $L(u)^\top L(u) = \|u\|_2^2 I_{16} - N_u$ and satisfies the universal cubic minimal polynomial $N_u^3 = 4\Lambda(u)^2 N_u$. Consequently, exact channel inversion $L(u)^{-1}$, Tikhonov/MMSE equalization, Moore--Penrose pseudoinversion $L(u)^+$ on the singular zero-divisor cone, and orthogonal projection $P_{\ker L(u)}$ collapse to 3-term polynomials in $N_u$ requiring only two octonion associators and zero SVD; (ii) \emph{The Co-Axial Multi-Jammer Theorem and Mutually-Annihilating Cascades:} the 84 primitive zero-divisor blades partition into 14 box-kite sextets over $S^6$ by their native associator axis $n(u) = (a'\times_{\Oc} b')/\Lambda(u)$, and $\dim(\ker L(u_1)\cap \ker L(u_2)) \in \{0, 2, 4\}$ is non-zero if and only if $n(u_1) = +n(u_2)$. When two jammers are non-coaxial ($\ker L(u_1)\cap \ker L(u_2) = \{0\}$), we construct a 2-stage cascade $(w_1, w_2)$ satisfying $w_1 \cdot w_2 = 0$ that annihilates every linear combination $J = \alpha u_1 + \beta u_2$ while transmitting desired signals strictly via the non-associative associator $(s\cdot w_1)\cdot w_2 = [s, w_1, w_2] \neq 0$; (iii) \emph{Pontryagin's Maximum Principle over $\Se$:} maximizing the control Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w) = \langle p, x\cdot w\rangle - \frac{\gamma}{2}\|J\cdot w\|_2^2 - \frac{\rho}{2}\|w\|_2^2$ yields a strictly concave quadratic program whose global maximizer $w^*$ is given in closed 3-term SVD-free form by $N_J$ applied to the Cayley--Dickson switching vector $\psi = x^*\cdot p$; (iv) \emph{Eisenstein $S_3$ Phase Automorphisms and the Sextic Invariant $J_3(u)$:} we prove that the $S_3$ factor in $\Aut(\Se) \cong G_2 \times S_3$ arises from cube-root-of-unity phase rotations $Z \mapsto e^{2\pi i/3}Z$ ($\bar{\omega}^2 = \omega \iff \omega^3 = 1$) and construct a sextic $G_2\times S_3$ invariant $J_3(u) = \RePart(q^3)/|q|^3$ that separates lower-octonion ($J_3 = +1$) from twisted-sector ($J_3 = -1$) excitations sharing identical singular spectra; and (v) \emph{Hurwitz--Radon 9-Frame Isometries and Extremal $[16, 8, 6]_3$ Ternary FEC:} the 9D subspace $\mathrm{span}\{e_0, \dots, e_8\}$ preserves Euclidean norm against arbitrary zero-divisor keys and reduces modulo $3$ to an extremal self-dual $[16, 8, 6]_3$ ternary code correcting all 1- and 2-trit errors on a $64\,\mathrm{MHz}$ Cortex-M4F microcontroller.

\section{Introduction: Five Paradoxes Beyond the Hurwitz and Moufang Thresholds}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==============================================================================
\IEEEPARstart{H}{urwitz's} celebrated 1898 composition theorem establishes that $\R$ ($\dim 1$), $\C$ ($\dim 2$), $\Ha$ ($\dim 4$), and $\Oc$ ($\dim 8$) are the only normed division algebras over the real numbers \cite{Baez2002}. At each step of the Cayley--Dickson doubling tower $\mathbb{A}_n = \mathbb{A}_{n-1} \oplus \mathbb{A}_{n-1} e_{2^{n-1}}$, a classical algebraic symmetry is surrendered: $\C \to \Ha$ surrenders commutativity ($xy \neq yx$); $\Ha \to \Oc$ surrenders associativity ($[x,y,z] \triangleq (xy)z - x(yz) \neq 0$) while retaining \emph{alternativity} ($[x,x,y] = [y,x,x] = 0$) and the Moufang identities; and $\Oc \to \Se = \mathbb{A}_4$ ($\dim 16$) surrenders alternativity ($u(uy) \neq u^2 y$) and norm composition ($\|uy\|_2 \neq \|u\|_2\|y\|_2$), giving birth to Moreno's 11-dimensional Stiefel cone of unit-norm zero divisors \cite{Moreno1998}.

In the mathematical physics and signal processing literature, the loss of alternativity at $\dim 16$ has long been viewed as an algebraic catastrophe: because $u^*(uy) \neq \|u\|_2^2 y$, standard Cayley--Dickson division $u^{-1} = u^*/\|u\|_2^2$ fails to invert the left multiplication operator $L(u): y \mapsto u\cdot y$. Yet when we examine the operator structure of $\Se$ from first principles, what appears to be algebraic pathology turns out to be a remarkably rigid, finite-degree operator calculus with capabilities that are \emph{provably impossible} in any associative or alternative algebra.

Specifically, this paper resolves five foundational questions at the intersection of non-associative algebra, optimal control, and embedded radio-frequency (RF) signal processing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Mutually-Annihilating Cascade Paradox:} Can two linear filter stages $w_1, w_2$ that annihilate each other ($w_1 \cdot w_2 = 0$) be cascaded to null multi-mode interference while allowing a desired signal $s$ to pass through unattenuated? In any associative algebra, $(s\cdot w_1)\cdot w_2 = s\cdot(w_1\cdot w_2) = 0$ forces total signal collapse. In $\Se$, we prove that $(s\cdot w_1)\cdot w_2 = [s, w_1, w_2]$, allowing the desired signal to propagate \emph{purely through the non-associative associator} while two non-coaxial jammers are simultaneously driven to exact zero.
    \item \textbf{The Cubic Alternativity-Defect Calculus $N_u$:} Why does $u^*(uy) \neq \|u\|_2^2 y$ in $\Se$, and can $L(u)$ be inverted or equalized without $\mathcal{O}(16^3)$ matrix inversion or SVD? We prove that left and right alternativity fail by the \emph{exact same symmetric linear operator} $N_u = N_u^\top$, which satisfies the universal degree-3 polynomial $N_u^3 = 4\Lambda(u)^2 N_u$. Every $16\times 16$ inverse, MMSE equalizer, Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse $L(u)^+$, and nullspace projector $P_{\ker L(u)}$ reduces to a 3-term closed-form polynomial in $N_u$.
    \item \textbf{The Co-Axial Zero-Divisor Plane Paradox:} Given two zero divisors $u_1 = e_1 + e_{10}$ and $u_2 = e_3 + e_{12}$, \emph{every} non-zero linear combination $\alpha u_1 + \beta u_2$ in their 2D span is a zero divisor with a 4D nullspace---yet $\ker L(u_1) \cap \ker L(u_2) = \{0\}$! We prove a complete classification of multi-jammer kernel intersections via the $S^6$ box-kite axis fibration $n(u) = (a'\times_{\Oc} b')/\Lambda(u)$.
    \item \textbf{Pontryagin's Maximum Principle on $\Se$:} How do we maximize the bilinear control Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w) = \langle p, x\cdot w\rangle - \frac{\gamma}{2}\|J\cdot w\|_2^2 - \frac{\rho}{2}\|w\|_2^2$ with respect to the control $w \in \Se$ under non-associative jammer dynamics? We prove that the Hessian $Q_{\gamma,\rho}(J) = (\rho + \gamma\|J\|_2^2)I_{16} - \gamma N_J$ is strictly positive definite for all $\rho > 0, \gamma \ge 0$, yielding an exact 3-term SVD-free global maximizer $w^*$.
    \item \textbf{Eisenstein $S_3$ Phase Symmetry, Sextic Invariants, and Hurwitz--Radon Ternary Codes:} Why is $\Aut(\Se) \cong G_2 \times S_3$, how can one distinguish lower-octonion ($e_1$) from twisted-sector ($e_9$) states that possess identical singular spectra, and how does the 9D Hurwitz--Radon subspace $\mathrm{span}\{e_0,\dots,e_8\}$ generate both rate-$9/16$ zero-divisor-immune spread spectrum and an extremal $[16, 8, 6]_3$ self-dual ternary code?
\end{enumerate}

\section{The $\C \oplus \C^7$ Model, Convention Census, and $\Aut(\Se) \cong G_2 \times S_3$}
\label{sec:automorphisms}
% ==============================================================================
Write $x = (a, b) \in \Oc \oplus \Oc e_8$ with imaginary parts $a' = \ImPart(a) \in \R^7$ and $b' = \ImPart(b) \in \R^7$. Under the Dickson--Moreno doubling rule
\begin{equation}
    (a, b)\cdot (c, d) = \bigl(ac - d^* b,\; da + b c^*\bigr),
    \label{eq:dickson_moreno}
\end{equation}
identifying $x \leftrightarrow (\alpha, Z) \in \C \oplus \C^7$ via the complex nucleus coordinate $\alpha = x_0 + i x_8 \in \C$ and the complexified Fano vector $Z = a' + i b' \in \C^7$ (where $i$ acts as right multiplication by $e_8$) collapses 16D sedenion multiplication into a transparent vector identity:
\begin{equation}
    (\alpha, Z)\cdot(\beta, W) = \Bigl(\alpha\beta - \bar{Z}\cdot W,\;\; \bar{\alpha} W + \beta Z + \overline{Z \times_{\C} W}\Bigr),
    \label{eq:c_c7_model}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{Z}\cdot W = \sum_{k=1}^7 \bar{Z}_k W_k$ is the Hermitian inner product and $Z \times_{\C} W$ is the $\C$-bilinear extension of the 7D octonionic Fano cross product.

\subsection{The Quaternion Trap and Opposite-Algebra Isomorphism}
In software and hardware implementations of Cayley--Dickson algebras, a recurring source of silent bugs is the choice of conjugation placement in the doubling template $(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - X(b,d),\, Y(a,d) + Z(b,c))$. Across all $64$ unital sign-free templates where $X, Y, Z$ independently apply or omit conjugation in either factor order:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Quaternion Trap:} Exactly $16$ of the $64$ templates produce the valid associative quaternion algebra $\Ha$ at $\C \to \Ha$ (because $\C$ is commutative, hiding reversed factor orders), but $14$ of those $16$ fail norm composition at $\Ha \to \Oc$.
    \item \textbf{Uniqueness at $\Oc$ and $\Se$:} Only $2$ of the $64$ templates yield a normed division algebra at $\Ha \to \Oc$: the Dickson--Moreno convention \eqref{eq:dickson_moreno} and the Baez--Schafer convention $(ac - d b^*, a^* d + c b)$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Opposite-Algebra Isomorphism and $S_3$ Reflection Automorphism; Lean~4 Theorem~5.44]
\label{thm:opposite_and_s3}
Across all $16 \times 16 = 256$ basis pairs $(e_i, e_j) \in \Se \times \Se$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Conjugation $x \mapsto x^*$ is an exact anti-automorphism $(x \cdot y)^* = y^* \cdot x^*$, establishing that the Baez--Schafer multiplication $x \cdot_{\mathrm{BS}} y = y \cdot_{\mathrm{DM}} x$ is the exact opposite algebra $\Se^{\mathrm{op}}$ on the same basis and preserves the $8 + 7$ $\PG(3,2)$ hyperplane partition identically.
    \item The reflection $\varepsilon: (a, b) \mapsto (a, -b)$ is an exact algebra automorphism $\varepsilon(x\cdot y) = \varepsilon(x)\cdot \varepsilon(y)$ fixing $\Oc$ and negating $\Oc e_8$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Why $\Aut(\Se) \cong G_2 \times S_3$: The Eisenstein Cube-Root Phase Law}
By Brown and Schafer's theorem, the automorphism group of the sedenions is $\Aut(\Se) \cong G_2 \times S_3$ ($\dim G_2 = 14$). A widespread misconception is that the $S_3$ factor originates from $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ triality. In fact, octonionic triality is \emph{not} an automorphism of $\Se$; the $S_3$ factor comes directly from the $\C \oplus \C^7$ multiplication law \eqref{eq:c_c7_model}.

Specifically, consider scaling the complexified Fano vector by a unit phase $Z \mapsto \omega Z$ ($|\omega| = 1$) while fixing $\alpha \in \C$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item In the scalar slot of \eqref{eq:c_c7_model}, $\overline{(\omega Z)}\cdot(\omega W) = |\omega|^2 \bar{Z}\cdot W = \bar{Z}\cdot W$ is invariant for \emph{all} $\omega \in U(1)$.
    \item In the vector slot, $\bar{\alpha}(\omega W) + \beta(\omega Z) = \omega(\bar{\alpha}W + \beta Z)$, whereas the conjugate cross product transforms as
    \begin{equation}
        \overline{(\omega Z) \times_{\C} (\omega W)} = \bar{\omega}^2 \overline{Z \times_{\C} W}.
    \end{equation}
    Hence $Z \mapsto \omega Z$ is an algebra automorphism of $\Se$ if and only if $\bar{\omega}^2 = \omega \iff \omega^3 = 1$!
\end{itemize}
Setting $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3} = -\frac{1}{2} + i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ yields the explicit order-3 automorphism $\psi \in \Aut(\Se)$ ($\psi^3 = \mathrm{id}$, $\varepsilon\psi\varepsilon = \psi^{-1}$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \psi(e_0) &= e_0, \qquad \psi(e_8) = e_8, \\
    \psi(e_k) &= -\frac{1}{2}e_k + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}e_{k+8}, \\
    \psi(e_{k+8}) &= -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}e_k - \frac{1}{2}e_{k+8} \qquad (k = 1,\dots,7).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:psi_s3}
\end{equation}
Together with $\varepsilon(Z) = \bar{Z}$, $\langle \varepsilon, \psi\rangle \cong S_3$ commutes elementwise with the diagonal $G_2$ action $(a,b) \mapsto (g(a), g(b))$.

\subsection{The Sextic $G_2 \times S_3$ Invariant $J_3(u)$}
Under $Z \mapsto e^{2\pi i/3} Z$ and $Z \mapsto \bar{Z}$, the complex scalar
\begin{equation}
    q(u) \triangleq \bar{Z}\cdot \bar{Z} = \bigl(\|a'\|_2^2 - \|b'\|_2^2\bigr) - 2i\langle a', b'\rangle
\end{equation}
transforms as $q \mapsto e^{-4\pi i/3} q = e^{2\pi i/3} q$ and $q \mapsto \bar{q}$. Consequently, $\RePart(q)$ is \emph{not} $S_3$-invariant, and the singular values of $L(u)$ depend only on $|q| = \sqrt{\|a'\|_2^4 + \|b'\|_2^4 + 2\langle a',b'\rangle^2 - 2\Lambda(u)^2}$, making the singular spectrum blind to the sign of $\|a'\|_2^2 - \|b'\|_2^2$. However, cubing $q$ kills the cube root of unity ($e^{6\pi i/3} = 1$), yielding a normalized \emph{sextic $G_2 \times S_3$ invariant}:
\begin{equation}
    J_3(u) \triangleq \frac{\RePart\bigl(q(u)^3\bigr)}{|q(u)|^3} = \frac{\Delta\bigl(\Delta^2 - 12\langle a', b'\rangle^2\bigr)}{\bigl(\Delta^2 + 4\langle a', b'\rangle^2\bigr)^{3/2}} \in [-1, +1],
    \label{eq:j3_def}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta = \|a'\|_2^2 - \|b'\|_2^2$. As proven in Lean~4 Theorem~5.45, $J_3(u)$ is strictly invariant under all of $\Aut(\Se) = G_2 \times S_3$ while cleanly separating lower-octonion basis states ($J_3(e_k) = +1$) from twisted-sector states ($J_3(e_{k+8}) = -1$) that possess identical singular values $\{1^{(16)}\}$.

\section{The Symmetric Alternativity-Defect Calculus $N_u$ and 3-Term SVD-Free Inverses}
\label{sec:nu_calculus}

For any $u = (a, b) \in \Se$, define the non-associative wedge magnitude
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \Lambda(u) &\triangleq \|a' \times_{\Oc} b'\|_2 = \sqrt{\|a'\|_2^2 \|b'\|_2^2 - \langle a', b'\rangle^2} \\
               &= \tfrac{1}{2}\|\Anative(u)\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Crucially, \emph{neither $u_0$ nor $u_8$ enters $\Lambda(u)$}: both belong to the associative complex nucleus $\C = \mathrm{span}\{e_0, e_8\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Symmetric Alternativity-Defect Operator $N_u$ and Cubic Minimal Polynomial; Lean~4 Theorems~5.37--5.38]
\label{thm:nu_cubic}
For every $u = (a, b) \in \Se$ and $y = (c, d) \in \Se$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Coincidence and Symmetry of Left/Right Alternativity Defects:} Left and right alternativity fail by the exact same symmetric linear operator $N_u = N_u^\top : \R^{16} \to \R^{16}$:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        N_u(y) &\triangleq u(uy) - u^2 y = (yu)u - y u^2 \\
               &= \Bigl([a, b, d]_{\Oc},\; -[a, b, c]_{\Oc}\Bigr),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:nu_def}
    \end{equation}
    where $[a, b, x]_{\Oc} = (ab)x - a(bx)$ is the octonion associator.
    \item \textbf{Exact Norm-Composition Defect:} For all $u, y \in \Se$,
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \|u \cdot y\|_2^2 &= \|u\|_2^2 \|y\|_2^2 - \langle y, N_u(y)\rangle \\
                          &= \|u\|_2^2 \|y\|_2^2 + 2\bigl\langle [a, b, c]_{\Oc},\, d\bigr\rangle.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:norm_defect}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Normal Equations and Universal Singular Spectrum:} Setting $s = \|u\|_2^2$, the Gram operators of left and right multiplication coincide:
    \begin{equation}
        L(u)^\top L(u) = R(u)^\top R(u) = s I_{16} - N_u.
        \label{eq:gram_nu}
    \end{equation}
    Moreover, $N_u$ vanishes on the 8D quaternion-hinge subspace $H_u \oplus H_u e_8$ ($H_u = \mathrm{span}\{e_0, a', b', a'\times_{\Oc} b'\}$) and satisfies the cubic identity
    \begin{equation}
        N_u^3 = 4\Lambda(u)^2 N_u.
        \label{eq:nu_cubic}
    \end{equation}
    Consequently, the eigenvalues of $N_u$ are $\bigl\{0^{(8)},\, +2\Lambda(u)^{(4)},\, -2\Lambda(u)^{(4)}\bigr\}$, and the squared singular values of $L(u)$ are universally

    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\label{sec:cascades}

Every zero divisor $u = (a', b') \in V_2(\R^7)$ defines a unit \emph{box-kite axis} on the 6-sphere $S^6 \subset \ImPart(\Oc)$:
\begin{equation}
    n(u) \triangleq \frac{a' \times_{\Oc} b'}{\Lambda(u)} = -\frac{\Anative(u)}{\|\Anative(u)\|_2} \in S^6.
    \label{eq:box_kite_axis}
\end{equation}
For any unit frame $(a', b') \in V_2(\R^7)$ and any $p \in \ImPart(\Oc)$ with $p \perp \{a', b', n(u)\}$, octonion alternativity and anticommutativity yield $(a' \cdot p)\cdot b' = -n(u)\cdot p = p \cdot n(u)$, uniting the Moreno partner map $p \mapsto (a' p) b'$ (\path{SedenionReal.nullspace} in \cite{Agourakis2026PG32}) with the axis-only parameterization
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \ker L(u) &= \ker R(u) \\
              &= \Bigl\{\, (p, -n(u)\cdot p) \;\Big|\; p \in \R^7,\; p \perp \{a', b', n(u)\} \,\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ker_param}
\end{equation}
As certified in Lean~4 Theorem~5.45, the 84 primitive zero-divisor blades $e_a \pm e_b$ ($a \in \{1..7\}, b \in \{9..15\}, b \neq a \oplus 8$) fiber over the 14 signed Fano axes $\pm e_m$ ($m = a \oplus b \oplus 8 \in \{1..7\}$) into \textbf{14 box-kite sextets} of 6 blades each ($14 \times 6 = 84$).

\subsection{The Co-Axial Multi-Jammer Nulling Theorem}
Suppose a receiver faces two simultaneous zero-divisor jammers $u_1, u_2 \in V_2(\R^7)$. When can a \emph{single} non-zero weight vector $w \in \Se$ annihilate both ($u_1 \cdot w = u_2 \cdot w = 0$)?

\begin{theorem}[Co-Axial Kernel Intersection Dichotomy; Lean~4 Theorem~5.39]
\label{thm:coaxial}
For any two zero divisors $u_1, u_2 \in V_2(\R^7)$, the intersection $\ker L(u_1) \cap \ker L(u_2)$ is a complex subspace under right multiplication by $e_8$, so its real dimension belongs strictly to $\{0, 2, 4\}$. Moreover:
\begin{equation}
    \ker L(u_1) \cap \ker L(u_2) \neq \{0\} \iff n(u_1) = +n(u_2).
\end{equation}
When $n(u_1) = n(u_2) = n$ and $\mathrm{span}\{a_1', b_1'\} \neq \mathrm{span}\{a_2', b_2'\}$, the intersection is 2-dimensional and spanned by the closed-form joint annihilator
\begin{equation}
    p_0 = (a_1' \times_{\Oc} a_2') - \langle a_1' \times_{\Oc} a_2',\, n\rangle n, \qquad w_{\mathrm{joint}} = \bigl(p_0,\; -n \cdot p_0\bigr).
    \label{eq:w_joint}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The Disjoint-Kernel Zero-Divisor Plane Paradox]
Consider $u_1 = e_1 + e_{10}$ (axis $n(u_1) = e_1 \times_{\Oc} e_2 = +e_3$) and $u_2 = e_3 + e_{12}$ (axis $n(u_2) = e_3 \times_{\Oc} e_4 = +e_7$). For \emph{every} $(\alpha, \beta) \neq (0, 0)$, the linear combination $u(\alpha, \beta) = \alpha u_1 + \beta u_2$ satisfies $\|\alpha e_1 + \beta e_3\|_2 = \|\alpha e_2 + \beta e_4\|_2$ and $\langle \alpha e_1 + \beta e_3, \alpha e_2 + \beta e_4\rangle = 0$; hence \emph{every vector in the 2D plane $\mathrm{span}\{u_1, u_2\}$ is a zero divisor with a 4D kernel}. Yet because $n(u_1) = e_3 \neq e_7 = n(u_2)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:coaxial} forces $\ker L(u_1) \cap \ker L(u_2) = \{0\}$! No single-stage weight can null both $u_1$ and $u_2$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Resolution via Mutually-Annihilating Non-Associative Cascades ($w_1 \cdot w_2 = 0$)}
To defeat two non-coaxial jammers $u_1, u_2$ when $\ker R(u_1) \cap \ker R(u_2) = \{0\}$, we exploit sequential parenthesization:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{out}} = \bigl(R \cdot w_1\bigr) \cdot w_2.
\end{equation}
Choose $w_1 \in \ker R(u_1)$ ($u_1 \cdot w_1 = 0$). Because $u_2$ is not co-axial with $u_1$, the Stage-1 residual $u_2' \triangleq u_2 \cdot w_1$ is non-zero. However, when $u_2'$ lies on the zero-divisor cone, $\dim\ker R(u_2') = 4$. Since $\dim\ker L(w_1) = 4$ as well, we can select a Stage-2 weight $w_2 \in \ker R(u_2') \cap \ker L(w_1)$ via the iterated projector $P_{\ker L(w_1)}\bigl(P_{\ker L(u_2')}(e_k)\bigr)$!

\begin{theorem}[Mutually-Annihilating 2-Stage Non-Associative Cascade; Lean~4 Theorem~5.39]
\label{thm:annihilating_cascade}
Let $u_1 = e_1 + e_{10}$ and $u_2 = e_1 + e_{13}$ be non-coaxial zero-divisor jammers ($n(u_1) = +e_3 \neq -e_4 = n(u_2)$). Define the primitive Stage-1 blade $w_1 = e_4 - e_{15} \in \ker R(u_1)$ and the 4-tap $\{\pm 1\}$ compound Stage-2 zero-divisor blade $w_2 = (e_1 + e_{10}) - (e_6 + e_{13}) \in \ker R(u_2 \cdot w_1) \cap \ker L(w_1)$ (whereas for antipodal axes $u_2 = e_1 - e_{10}$, the 2-tap blade $w_2 = e_1 + e_{10}$ suffices). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mutual Filter Annihilation:} The two filter stages annihilate each other:
    \begin{equation}
        w_1 \cdot w_2 = 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Total Dual-Jammer Cancellation:} For any linear combination $J = \alpha u_1 + \beta u_2$,
    \begin{equation}
        \bigl(J \cdot w_1\bigr) \cdot w_2 = \alpha \bigl(\underbrace{u_1 \cdot w_1}_{0}\bigr)\cdot w_2 + \beta \underbrace{\bigl(u_2 \cdot w_1\bigr)\cdot w_2}_{0} = 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Purely Associator-Borne Signal Transmission:} For the desired signal $s = e_2$, the cascaded output equals the sedenion associator $[s, w_1, w_2]$ and is strictly non-zero:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \bigl(e_2 \cdot w_1\bigr)\cdot w_2 &= e_2 \cdot \underbrace{\bigl(w_1 \cdot w_2\bigr)}_{0} + \bigl[e_2, w_1, w_2\bigr] \\
        &= \bigl[e_2, w_1, w_2\bigr], \quad \bigl\|\bigl(e_2 \cdot w_1\bigr)\cdot w_2\bigr\|_2^2 = 4.
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:annihilating_cascade} exhibits a phenomenon with no counterpart in linear or associative algebra: the effective end-to-end transfer operator $T(x) = (x\cdot w_1)\cdot w_2 = [x, w_1, w_2]$ is \emph{100\% non-associative}, vanishing identically if the underlying algebra were associative, yet delivering full rank-8 signal transmission while zeroing two non-coaxial jammers.

\section{Pontryagin's Maximum Principle over $\Se$ and $\PG(3,2)$}
\label{sec:pontryagin}
% ==============================================================================
Consider a controlled 16D sedenion RF state trajectory $\dot{x} = f(x) + x \cdot w$ with state $x \in \Se$, costate (adjoint vector) $p \in \Se$, jammer state $J \in \Se$ (weighted by $\gamma \ge 0$), and control effort weight $\rho > 0$. By Pontryagin's Maximum Principle (PMP) \cite{Pontryagin1962}, the optimal control $w^* \in \mathcal{U} \subseteq \Se$ maximizes the \emph{Pontryagin Control Hamiltonian}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w) \triangleq \langle p, x \cdot w\rangle - \frac{\gamma}{2}\|J \cdot w\|_2^2 - \frac{\rho}{2}\|w\|_2^2.
    \label{eq:pmp_hamiltonian}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Closed-Form Sedenion Pontryagin Maximum Principle; Lean~4 Theorems~5.42--5.43]
\label{thm:pontryagin}
For any state $x \in \Se$, costate $p \in \Se$, jammer $J \in \Se$, jammer penalty $\gamma \ge 0$, and effort weight $\rho > 0$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adjoint Switching Reduction and Strict Concavity:} By the Cayley--Dickson adjoint identity $L(x)^\top = L(x^*)$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:nu_cubic}, define the \emph{Pontryagin switching vector} $\psi(x, p) \triangleq x^* \cdot p$ and the symmetric Hessian operator
    \begin{equation}
        Q_{\gamma,\rho}(J) \triangleq \bigl(\rho + \gamma\|J\|_2^2\bigr)I_{16} - \gamma N_J.
        \label{eq:pmp_hessian}
    \end{equation}
    Then $\mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w) = \langle \psi, w\rangle - \frac{1}{2}\langle w, Q_{\gamma,\rho}(J)w\rangle$. Since $2\Lambda(J) \le \|J\|_2^2$, the minimum eigenvalue of $Q_{\gamma,\rho}(J)$ is $\rho + \gamma\bigl(\|J\|_2^2 - 2\Lambda(J)\bigr) \ge \rho > 0$; thus $\mathcal{H}$ is strictly concave on $\R^{16}$.
    \item \textbf{3-Term SVD-Free Unconstrained Global Maximizer ($\mathcal{U} = \Se$):} Let $\tau = \rho + \gamma\|J\|_2^2$. The unique global maximizer $w^* = \arg\max_{w \in \Se}\mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w) = Q_{\gamma,\rho}(J)^{-1}\psi$ is given in closed 3-term SVD-free form by
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        w^* &= \frac{1}{\tau}\psi + \frac{\gamma}{\tau^2 - 4\gamma^2\Lambda(J)^2}N_J(\psi) \\
            &\quad + \frac{\gamma^2}{\tau\bigl(\tau^2 - 4\gamma^2\Lambda(J)^2\bigr)}N_J^2(\psi),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:pmp_wstar}
    \end{equation}
    achieving the global maximum $\mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w^*) = \frac{1}{2}\langle \psi, w^*\rangle$, with exact perturbation deficit $\mathcal{H}(w^*) - \mathcal{H}(w^* + \delta) = \frac{1}{2}\langle \delta, Q_{\gamma,\rho}(J)\delta\rangle > 0$ for all $\delta \neq 0$.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Divisor Unit-Sphere Maximizer ($\gamma \to \infty$, $\mathcal{U} = \{w \in \ker L(J) : \|w\|_2 = 1\}$):} When $J \in V_2(\R^7)$ is a Moreno zero divisor ($\|J\|_2^2 = 2\Lambda(J)$), the constrained unit-norm control maximizing $\langle p, x\cdot w\rangle$ subject to exact jammer annihilation $J \cdot w = 0$ is
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        w^*_{\ker} &= \frac{P_{\ker L(J)}(x^* \cdot p)}{\bigl\|P_{\ker L(J)}(x^* \cdot p)\bigr\|_2}, \\
        \mathcal{H}^*_{\ker} &= \bigl\|P_{\ker L(J)}(x^* \cdot p)\bigr\|_2.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:pmp_zd_sphere}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

When the control is restricted to the discrete 168-element Pre-ADC analog crossbar alphabet $\mathcal{B}_{84}^{\pm} = \bigl\{\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_a \pm e_b)\bigr\}$, Pontryagin maximization reduces to the finite bang-bang selection $w^*_{\mathrm{BB}} = \arg\max_{w \in \mathcal{B}_{84}^{\pm}} \mathcal{H}(x, p, J, w)$, which automatically selects one of the 4 zero-divisor partners of $J$ whenever $\gamma \gg 1$.
