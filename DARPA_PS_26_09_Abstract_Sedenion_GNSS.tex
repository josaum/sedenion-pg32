\section{Problem and What We Claim}
Controlled Reception Pattern Antennas (CRPAs) on tactical GNSS receivers null jammers with MVDR/STAP weights $\mathbf{w}\propto\hat{\mathbf{R}}_{xx}^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{sat}}$, which require a covariance estimate over $K$ snapshots and an $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ inversion. This is the right tool when snapshots are plentiful: with $K\ge$ (number of interferers), our simulations show MVDR reaching 85--95~dB null depth against a $+60$~dB JNR jammer and \emph{outperforming every signature-driven nuller}, including ours. We do not claim otherwise.

What MVDR does not provide is (i) a weight update that is closed-form, inversion-free and representable in 16-bit fixed point with no multipliers; (ii) any guarantee about carrier-phase continuity when nulls are re-steered; (iii) adequate null depth when $K$ falls below the interferer count (at $K=2$ against 3 and 6 jammers we measure 21~dB and 10~dB respectively, versus 68~dB and 65~dB for a signature-driven nuller). The proposed effort delivers a null-steering stage that provides (i)--(iii), with its limits stated and proven rather than hidden.

\section{Technical Approach}
\subsection{Algebraic construction}
An 8-element complex baseband snapshot ($\mathbb{C}^8\cong\mathbb{R}^{16}$) is treated as an element of the 16-dimensional Cayley--Dickson sedenion algebra $\mathbb{S}$. Unlike $\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}$, $\mathbb{S}$ has zero divisors: for a jammer signature $u$ mapped onto the Moreno cone, the left-multiplication operator $L(u)$ has an exact 4-dimensional kernel with a \emph{closed-form} basis $v=(c,\,(ac)b)$ (Theorem 5.1 of [1], machine-checked in Lean~4/Mathlib). The nulling weight is obtained by a handful of octonion products---no covariance, no inversion, no iteration. Two structural properties follow and are both machine-checked:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=16pt,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Anti-partner gain:} any satellite signature in the orthogonal anti-partner direction is passed with exactly $+3.01$~dB coherent gain (measured $\sigma=0$ over 256 trials).
    \item \textbf{Phase-continuous null switching:} the 15 Fano hyperplanes of $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$ pairwise intersect in a quaternion subalgebra $\mathbb{H}_{L_{12}}$ on which the associator vanishes identically (\texttt{gnss\_pll\_hinge\_associative}, checked over all 56 workspace transitions). Routing the carrier reference through the hinge gives $0.0000^\circ$ phase jump at a null switch; the unrouted sedenion filter gives $58^\circ$ and MVDR re-estimation $5.8^\circ$ with a 10\% cycle-slip rate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The leakage bound: what null depth is actually achievable}
A nuller built from an estimated signature $\hat u$ sees the true signature $u=\hat u+\delta$. We prove (Lean~4, theorem \texttt{leakage\_bound}, standard Mathlib axioms only):
\[
    \hat u\cdot v = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \|(\hat u+\delta)\cdot v\|_2^2 \;\le\; 2\,\|\delta\|_2^2\,\|v\|_2^2 .
\]
Null depth is therefore governed by signature-estimation error, $\approx 20\log_{10}(1/\|\delta\|)-3$~dB, and \emph{no algebraic structure can beat this}. The measured sedenion residual sits within $1.4\times$ of the bound at every tested $\|\delta\|$ (Table~1); per output beam it matches an orthogonal-projection nuller built from the same $\hat u$ to within 2~dB. Figures such as ``139~dB'' or ``312~dB'' reported in our earlier simulations are the $\delta=0$ floating-point floor and are not performance claims.

\subsection{Operating regime}
Per-channel gain/phase mismatch of $(1\%,1^\circ)$ yields $\|\delta\|\approx0.02$ and a $\sim$34~dB (16-vector) / $\sim$50~dB (per-beam) null; $(0.1\%,0.1^\circ)$ yields $\sim$54 / $\sim$70~dB. Hence the method is calibration-limited, like all signature-driven nullers, and the proposed work is correspondingly centered on calibration and fixed-point realization rather than on depth claims. The regime where it is the right choice: fast interferer dynamics or dense multi-jammer scenes where $K<J$, SWaP budgets that exclude a floating-point inversion, and RTK/carrier-phase receivers where null re-steering must not slip the PLL.

\section{12-Month Plan and Deliverables}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=16pt,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Task 1 (Mo.\ 1--4) --- Calibration to the leakage bound.} Closed-loop pilot-tone and blind calibration of the $\mathbb{C}^8\!\to\!\mathbb{S}$ map under measured mutual coupling; target $\|\delta\|\le 3\times10^{-3}$ ($\ge$50~dB per-beam null) across L1/L2. \textit{D1:} calibration algorithm and error budget tied to the proven bound.
    \item \textbf{Task 2 (Mo.\ 3--8) --- Fixed-point RTL.} 16-bit Q14 pipeline on Zynq UltraScale+ RFSoC implementing the cone projection, nullspace partner, and 35 hinge projections; our Q14 model shows 0/256 sign-table mismatches and $\sim$75~dB quantization-limited depth with no DSP48 usage. \textit{D2:} SystemVerilog IP, bit-true test suite, and a Lean-to-RTL conformance check of the sign table.
    \item \textbf{Task 3 (Mo.\ 7--12) --- Live-RF HIL.} 8-channel phase-coherent SDR testbed with a GNSS constellation simulator under wideband, chirp and repeater interference; MVDR/LSMI run side-by-side on the same captures. \textit{D3:} data package, final report, and a published regime map (null depth vs.\ $K$, $J$, $\|\delta\|$) for both methods.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Risk:} the principal risk is that achievable $\|\delta\|$ on real hardware is $>10^{-2}$, capping per-beam depth near 50~dB. This is a known, bounded outcome, not a failure mode; the Task~3 regime map is the deliverable either way. \textbf{Transition:} GPR on all RTL and calibration code; integration path via a U.S.\ prime for M-code CRPA retrofits. The proposer is a foreign entity and will team with a U.S.\ organization as required for any follow-on involving controlled technical data.
